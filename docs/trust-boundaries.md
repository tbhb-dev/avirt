# Status: planned

This document describes the planned design in [components](./components.md), with the connections and their transports in [networking](./networking.md). The implemented system's boundaries are in [workload hardening](./workload-hardening.md), [workload topology](./workload-topology.md) and [identity](./identity.md).

Every listener that a caller reaches across a container or host boundary requires mutual TLS with SPIFFE identities from the first byte, and authorizes the caller's exact identity before reading an application byte. Authentication never grants access by itself: each listener then authorizes the operation against the caller's workload, generation and role. Host management and cross-workload access are denied unless a rule below allows them.

## Local exceptions

These listeners inside the workload container do not use mutual TLS. Each one is reachable only from inside the workload container, and each fixes the principal and the operations of every connection it accepts:

- avirtnetd's forward proxy, which accepts only `CONNECT` and forwards every connection to avirtmedproxyd for policy;
- the DNS stub resolver, which forwards every query to avirtmedproxyd for policy;
- the agent sockets, which carry only avirtguest and avirtmcp traffic, under fixed principals;
- the local service sockets and the launcher sockets, which only the helper UIDs can open.

On the host, these local listeners do not use mutual TLS either, and authenticate the local caller instead:

- avirtedgeproxyd's local client listener `edgeproxy.sock`, a user-owned 0600 socket in the runtime directory;
- avirthostd's `admin.sock`, which admits only avirthostd's own UID or root by peer credentials (see [admin CLI](./admin-cli.md)).

uid 1000 never holds an SVID or its key, the SPIFFE bundle, a CA key or a real credential.

## Compromise scope

| Compromised | Can reach | Cannot reach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agent (uid 1000) | avirtnetd's forward proxy and the DNS stub, subject to the workload's policy in avirtmedproxyd and the host policy snapshot and private-address block in avirtedgeproxyd. DNS as an exfiltration path, bounded by `resolve` policy and logged. The agent sockets, as `guest-cli` and as the workload's MCP client. Through `guest-cli`, the workload management operations its policy grants, within the constraints in [workload management](./components.md#workload-management): never more authority than the workload holds, no reserved labels, no host administration. Its own files and the workspace. The placeholders and the MITM CA certificate, which are not secrets. | Any SVID or key, the helper sockets, the memory of other UIDs, any host socket (`AF_VSOCK` is denied, and the workload VM's only host vsock listener is the one behind `/run/avirt/mediator.sock`, used by the runtime's in-guest socket forwarder), any network, any real credential, other workloads except through avirtedgeproxyd's policy. The filesystem limits are in [sandbox](./sandbox.md#guarantees-by-layer). |
| Guest root (the whole workload container) | Everything in the workload container, including the workload SVID in avirtnetd. Every HTTP/2 stream on the workload's connection to its mediator, as the workload and as every process in the container. The workload management operations the workload's policy grants, within the same constraints as for the agent. | The mediator's keys, the workload's MITM CA key, real credentials (they stay at avirtsecretsd and the gateways), host services directly (the workload's HTTP/2 connection to its mediator is its only connection out, and ends at avirtmedproxyd), a mediator SVID (avirthostd issues only `/ns/<ns>/workload/<uuid>` to a workload-authenticated request), another namespace's identity, other workloads except through avirtedgeproxyd's policy |
| Mediator (avirtmediatord, avirtmedproxyd or its Envoy) | Bypassing its own workload's policy and placeholder misuse check. The workload's MITM CA key, with which it can intercept the agent's TLS connections to the hosts within the CA's name constraints, including ones the policy routes through TLS passthrough, for this and every later generation of the workload. A workload whose policy permits no interception has no MITM CA. The workload's placeholders and mediator-injected short-lived credentials. Host services through avirthostd's SNI routing, as that workload only. External endpoints, other workloads, services and host-local services that the host policy snapshot allows for its own identity, all through avirtedgeproxyd. Forging its own workload's mediator-reported events. | Model and MCP credentials (they never leave the gateways), other workloads' credentials (the broker authorizes by workload and generation), private and host addresses (avirtedgeproxyd checks every resolved address), a workload SVID for its own key (rotation requires the current workload key), any other mediator directly (SNI routing never connects two mediators), the SPIFFE CA. A compromised Envoy alone also cannot reach avirtmediatord's key or the control routes on the mediator's connection to avirthostd. |
| avirtedgeproxyd | Every workload-to-workload, service, host-local service and external connection on the host, as the policy enforcement point for all of them. Asserting any caller identity in `x-avirt-caller` to destination mediators, avirtapid and peers. Authorized host-local backends. Its own SVID and the peer bundles it holds. | The SPIFFE CA, model and MCP credentials (they stay at the gateways and avirtsecretsd), any mediator's or workload's keys, the plaintext of the TLS connections a mediator opens to the upstream and of workload-to-workload bytes, avirt's host services except through their own authorization |
| Build job (guest root in the build VM) | Its own inputs, its staging repository, its cache scope, and what its workload policy snapshot allows through avirtedgeproxyd. Writing anything into its staging output. | Any workspace repository, tag or annotation (only avirtbuilderd promotes), another build's inputs or staging, real credentials, avirtapid (avirthostd refuses a build job's `guest-cli` stream), avirthostd's control API, any signing key |
| avirtregistryd | Every stored workspace image, and the registry credentials it obtains from avirtsecretsd. Serving a forged index or forged content to avirthostd. | Running content: avirthostd verifies every digest it pulls against the resolved manifest, so forged content fails at launch, while a forged resolution is bounded by `launch_workspace` on the image record it returns |
| avirtbuilderd | Starting build jobs, promoting what it chooses, and writing any annotation | Running commands on the host, any SVID other than its own, avirthostd's control API beyond creating build jobs |
| Peer | This host's peer listener, as identities in its own trust domain. Local published ports and services that this host's host policy snapshot allows for those identities, and the avirtapid routes its enrollment allows (see [peering](./peering.md#what-crosses-a-peering)). | This host's SVIDs or CA (its bundle only verifies), identities in any other trust domain, any mediator or host service other than through avirtedgeproxyd, any destination the host policy snapshot does not allow for its identities |

The rows above describe enforce mode. In audit mode, a policy decision with no permit or with a forbid is logged as `would-deny` and allowed, so a destination the policy snapshot does not permit is reachable. These hold in both modes: caller authentication, generation fencing, name-to-entity resolution, the private-address block, `Host` matching, the placeholder misuse check, credential bindings, `intercept` decisions and `resolve` decisions.

The `guest-cli` principal and the workload's MCP access are inside the agent's trust boundary. avirtguest and avirtmcp run as uid 1000, so anything granted to them is granted to the agent.

Runtime isolation bounds a guest-root compromise. On Docker Desktop every workload shares one kernel, so a kernel escape from any workload container reaches every workload's container and mediator (see [compute runtimes](./compute-runtimes.md#docker-desktop-runtime)).

## Event provenance

Every event in a workload log carries `workload`, `gen` and `seq`, and a `src` label naming the component that observed it. avirthostd stamps `src`, `workload` and `gen` from the authenticated connection the event arrived on, and ignores any value the producer supplied. The label states how far the event can be trusted.

| `src` | Written by | Trust |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `hostd` | avirthostd, from its own observation (launch, runtime exits, write-backs, errors) | Host-observed |
| `edgeproxy` | libavirtenvoy in avirtedgeproxyd's Envoy, for connections it opened | Host-observed |
| `gateway` | avirtmodelgwd and avirtmcpgwd, for requests they served | Host-observed |
| `secrets` | avirtsecretsd, for credential fetches, refreshes and provider errors it observed | Host-observed |
| `mediator` | libavirtenvoy in avirtmedproxyd's Envoy, avirtmedproxyd and avirtmediatord | Mediator-reported: trustworthy only while the mediator is not compromised |
| `workload` | avirtnetd, avirtinitd and the main workload process, on the `/envelopes` stream | Workload-reported and relayed by the mediator: trustworthy only while neither guest root nor the mediator is compromised |

avirtauditd keeps `src` in every OCSF record it produces.
